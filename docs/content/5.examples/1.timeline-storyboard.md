## What's happening

Each card plays its own short move, independent of the other three. Those
moves sit on one shared timeline, like clips on a video track. The four
finish together in a ripple. The scrub bar moves through all of it at once.
Early on, Card 02 is already moving while Card 03 has not started.

## How it's built

Each card gets its own [`useAnimeTimeline`](/composables/use-anime-timeline)
with `autoplay: false`, so its keyframes can be tuned without the animation
playing. The closing wave is a fifth timeline of the same kind, staggered
across all four boxes at once.

Those five go onto a sixth, master timeline with
`.sync(childTimeline, offsetMs)`. The offset sets how far into the master that
routine starts. Offsets climb (0ms, 350ms, 850ms, 1450ms) so each card starts
after the last. The wave lands at 2300ms once the solos finish.

From there, the controls only talk to the master timeline. Its
`.seek(progress * duration)` scrubs every card, since they all run on the
same clock.

## Building it yourself

1. Give each card its own [`useAnimeTimeline`](/composables/use-anime-timeline) with `autoplay: false`, and author its keyframes independently.
2. Optionally split a card into an outer element for position and an inner one for scale and rotation. This keeps each `.add()` schedule independent when position and rotation steps have different durations.
3. Add one more `autoplay: false` timeline for the closing move, using `stagger()` on the shared selector rather than per-card offsets.
4. Create the master timeline, also `autoplay: false`, with `onUpdate` for progress and `onBegin` / `onPause` / `onComplete` for the play state.
5. Call `.sync(childTimeline, offsetMs)` on the master for each routine, climbing the offsets so they play in sequence. Sync the wave last so it lands after the solos finish.
6. Wire Play, Pause, and Restart to the master's `.play()`, `.pause()`, and `.restart()`, and a range slider to `.seek(value * master.duration)`.
