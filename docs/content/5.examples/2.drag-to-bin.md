## What's happening

A card shrinks when its centre moves over the bin. The bin grows a little
and its outline turns to the primary colour. A card released over the bin
shrinks the rest of the way and fades out. The cards to its right then
slide left with a small overshoot. A card pulled back out before release returns
to full size. A card released anywhere else springs back to its slot.
Restored cards pop in at their old places while the others make room.

## How it's built

Each card is dragged with [`useDraggable`](/composables/use-draggable).
`snap: [0]` sends a released card back to its origin, and
`releaseEase: spring({ bounce: 0.35, duration: 500 })` makes that return
overshoot and settle. A card that misses the bin needs no release code.

The hit test measures the bin and the card's rest centre once, in
`onGrab`. `onUpdate` adds the draggable's `x` and `y` to that centre and
checks whether the point is inside the bin. Nothing else moves during a
drag, so one measurement per grab is enough.

The card's scale and the bin's scale run through
[`useAnimatable`](/composables/use-animatable) with a `spring()` ease. The
card goes to `0.6` over the bin and the bin to `1.04`. An animatable
retargets from its current value, so a card that moves in and out of the
bin quickly never jumps.

A drop happens in two stages. First, `onRelease` calls `draggable.stop()`
to cancel the return spring. The card sits inside
[`<AnimeTransition>`](/components/transitions) under a `v-if`, and hiding it
plays the leave: scale to `0` and opacity to `0` in 250ms. The card is
removed from the list only after that leave ends. The order matters
because each card's draggable belongs to its own card component.
`useDraggable` reverts its draggable when that component is disposed, and
the revert moves the card back to its origin. A card removed from the list
while it still sat in the bin would jump back to the row before fading.

The row is an [`<AnimeTransitionGroup>`](/components/transitions) of those
card components. Removing a card fades out its empty slot. The slot leaves
the flow as its leave starts, so the cards to its right move straight
away. Their `moveAnimation` uses a `spring()` ease. Restored cards scale
in from `0.6` on a spring too.

The row and the bin sit in grid columns, with the bin's column a fixed
width at the right end. During a move, AnimeJS
[layout](https://animejs.com/documentation/layout) sets the group's own
width and height inline and clears its `max-width`. A group sized with
`flex-1` and `max-w-*` stretches for the length of each move and pushes a
bin beside it sideways. The grid keeps the bin in place however many cards
are left.

## Building it yourself

1. Keep the cards in a `ref` array of `{ id, name, icon }`. Restore sets it back to the full list.
2. Lay out a stage with `grid h-28 grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)_5rem] gap-3 sm:grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)_8rem]`, with the row in the first column and the bin in the second.
3. Render `<AnimeTransitionGroup tag="ul" class="relative flex items-center gap-2">` with a card component per item, keyed by `id`.
4. Pass `enterAnimation` as `{ opacity: [0, 1], scale: [0.6, 1], ease: spring({ bounce: 0.4, duration: 500 }) }`, `leaveAnimation` as `{ opacity: 0, duration: 200, ease: 'out(2)' }` and `moveAnimation` as `{ ease: spring({ bounce: 0.35, duration: 450 }) }`, with `spring` from `#nanime/easings`.
5. In the card component, render an `li` slot with a fixed size, `h-16 w-12` on phones and `size-20` from `sm`, so four cards and the bin fit in one row. Inside it, put an `<AnimeTransition>` around the card element, shown with `v-if="!dropped"`.
6. Call `useDraggable(card, { snap: [0], releaseEase, onGrab, onUpdate, onRelease })` on the card element.
7. In `onGrab`, read the bin's rect and the card's rect. Store the card's centre minus `draggable.x` and `draggable.y` as its rest centre.
8. In `onUpdate`, add `draggable.x` and `draggable.y` to the rest centre, test the point against the bin's rect, and act only when the result changes.
9. When it changes, call `useAnimatable(card, { scale: { ease: spring(...) } })`'s `scale(0.6)` or `scale(1)`, and emit the result to the parent. The parent sets the bin's highlight and calls its own animatable's `scale(1.04)` or `scale(1)`.
10. In `onRelease`, if the card is over the bin, call `draggable.stop()` and set `dropped` to `true`. On the transition's `@after-leave`, emit an event that removes the item from the parent's array.
