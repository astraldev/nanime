onto the bin deletes it, and the cards to its right slide left to fill its
place. A card dropped anywhere else springs back. Restore brings the
deleted cards back.

## What's happening

A card grows to fill the bin when its centre moves over it, and stays
centred on the bin while the pointer moves inside. The bin's neutral
background and outline get a little stronger. A card pulled back out
shrinks to its own size and follows the pointer again. A card released
over the bin fades out there. The cards to its right then slide left with
a small overshoot. A card released anywhere else springs back to its slot.
Restored cards pop in at their old places while the others make room.

## How it's built

Each card is dragged with [`useDraggable`](/composables/use-draggable).
`snap: [0]` sends a released card back to its origin, and
`releaseEase: spring({ bounce: 0.35, duration: 500 })` makes that return
overshoot and settle. A card that misses the bin needs no release code.

The hit test measures the bin and the card's rest centre once, in
`onGrab`. `onUpdate` adds the draggable's `x` and `y` to that centre and
checks whether the point is inside the bin. Nothing else moves during a
drag, so one measurement per grab is enough.

The fill is one number from `0` to `1`, animated by
[`useAnimatable`](/composables/use-animatable) on a plain object with a
`spring()` ease. The card's width, height and offset are interpolated from
it. At `0` the card has its own size. At `1` it covers the bin, less 6px
of padding, and sits on the bin's centre. `onUpdate` recomputes the offset
to the bin's centre on every move, so the card stays on the bin while the
pointer moves inside it. An animatable retargets from its current value,
so a card that moves in and out of the bin quickly never jumps.

A drop happens in two stages. First, `onRelease` calls `draggable.stop()`
to cancel the return spring, which leaves the card filling the bin. The
card sits inside [`<AnimeTransition>`](/components/transitions) under a
`v-if`, and hiding it plays the leave: a 200ms fade after a 250ms delay,
so a card dropped as it enters the bin finishes filling it first. The card
is removed from the row only after that leave ends. The order matters
because each card's draggable belongs to its own card component.
`useDraggable` reverts its draggable when that component is disposed, and
the revert moves the card back to its origin. A card removed from the row
while it still sat in the bin would jump back to the row before fading.

The row is an [`<AnimeTransitionGroup>`](/components/transitions) of those
card components. Removing a card fades out its empty slot. The slot leaves
the flow as its leave starts, so the cards to its right move straight
away. Their `moveAnimation` uses a `spring()` ease. Restored cards scale
in from `0.6` on a spring too.

The row and the bin sit in grid columns, with the bin's column a fixed
width at the right end. During a move, AnimeJS
[layout](https://animejs.com/documentation/layout) sets the group's own
width and height inline and clears its `max-width`. A group sized with
`flex-1` and `max-w-*` stretches for the length of each move and pushes a
bin beside it sideways. The grid keeps the bin in place however many cards
are left.

## Building it yourself

1. Keep the cards in a `ref` array of `{ id, name, icon }`. Restore sets it back to the full list.
2. Lay out a stage with `grid h-28 grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)_5rem] gap-3 sm:grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)_8rem]`, with the row in the first column and the bin in the second.
3. Render `<AnimeTransitionGroup tag="ul" class="relative flex items-center gap-2">` with a card component per item, keyed by `id`.
4. Pass `enterAnimation` as `{ opacity: [0, 1], scale: [0.6, 1], ease: spring({ bounce: 0.4, duration: 500 }) }`, `leaveAnimation` as `{ opacity: 0, duration: 200, ease: 'out(2)' }` and `moveAnimation` as `{ ease: spring({ bounce: 0.35, duration: 450 }) }`, with `spring` from `#nanime/easings`.
5. In the card component, render an `li` slot with a fixed size, `h-16 w-12` on phones and `size-20` from `sm`, so four cards and the bin fit in one row.
6. Inside the slot, put an `<AnimeTransition>` around a handle element shown with `v-if="!dropped"`. The handle holds the visible card. The draggable owns the handle's transform, so the card's size and offset go on the inner element.
7. Call `useDraggable(handle, { snap: [0], releaseEase, onGrab, onUpdate, onRelease })`.
8. In `onGrab`, read the bin's rect and the handle's rect. Store the handle's centre minus `draggable.x` and `draggable.y` as its rest centre, the handle's size, and the bin's size less the padding.
9. In `onUpdate`, add `draggable.x` and `draggable.y` to the rest centre and test the point against the bin's rect. While it is inside, store the vector from that point to the bin's centre.
10. When the result changes, call `progress(1)` or `progress(0)` on `useAnimatable(fill, { progress: { ease: spring(...) } })`, where `fill` is `reactive({ progress: 0 })`, and emit the result so the parent can set the bin's highlight.
11. Bind the inner card's `width`, `height` and `transform` in a `computed` interpolated from `fill.progress`, the two sizes and the stored vector.
12. In `onRelease`, if the card is over the bin, call `draggable.stop()` and set `dropped` to `true`. On the transition's `@after-leave`, emit an event that removes the item from the parent's array.
