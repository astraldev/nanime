A small media library of image, video and audio tiles. It plays a
sequence on a loop, one step every 2.8 seconds: the same tiles switch
between a row, a grid, a list and masonry columns, then get filtered to
images and back, shuffled, sorted, added to and removed from. The status line names the
current step and the next one, and clicking a tile expands it.

## What's happening

Every tile glides to its new place and grows or shrinks to its new size
when the layout changes. The icon and number inside each tile slide to
their new spots too, for example when a square tile becomes a list row.
New tiles pop in with a small overshoot. Removed tiles swell for a moment,
then shrink away while the tiles around them close the gap. An expanded
tile pushes its neighbours aside. A change that lands in the middle of a
move starts from wherever the tiles are at that moment.

## How it's built

The tiles are a `v-for` inside
[`<AnimeTransitionGroup>`](/components/transitions). The group runs
`enterAnimation` and `leaveAnimation` through AnimeJS, so they take
keyframes and eases. The enter scales each tile from `0` to `1` on a
`spring()` ease, so it overshoots and settles. The leave holds full
opacity for its first half and
scales up to `1.1` before shrinking to `0`.

Moves come from AnimeJS
[layout](https://animejs.com/documentation/layout). The
group records the position and size of every element inside it before each
update, then animates the difference after the update. CSS cannot
transition from `display: flex` to `display: grid` or into `columns-3`.
The group measures the result instead of the CSS, so the layout switch is
only a change of classes on the group. An expanded tile is the same thing
on one item: it gets `col-span-2 row-span-2` or a bigger size, and the
group animates it and its neighbours.

Leaving tiles are taken out of the flow when their leave starts, which
lets the rest move into the gap straight away. When an update lands during
a move, the group records the tiles where they are mid-flight and animates
from there.

`moveAnimation` uses a `spring()` ease too, so tiles snap into their new
places, with `stagger(15)` as its delay. The
layout counts every element in the group, the icon and number included, so
the delay grows along the list and each tile's content trails its tile by
a few milliseconds.

## Building it yourself

1. Keep the items in a `ref` array of `{ id, kind }`, and filter them with a `computed` for the current category.
2. Keep the current layout name in a `ref`, with a record that maps each name to the group's classes: `flex flex-wrap`, `grid grid-cols-4 auto-rows-[3.5rem] grid-flow-dense`, `flex flex-col` and `columns-3`.
3. Render `<AnimeTransitionGroup class="relative">` and bind the current layout's classes with `:class`. `position: relative` places leaving items against the group.
4. Render each item with `:key="item.id"`, and give it a class from its layout and from whether it is the expanded one. In the grid, the expanded item gets `col-span-2 row-span-2`.
5. Pass `enterAnimation` as `{ opacity: [0, 1], scale: [0, 1], ease: spring({ bounce: 0.4, duration: 500 }) }`, with `spring` from `#nanime/easings`, and `leaveAnimation` as params with keyframes.
6. Pass `moveAnimation` as `{ delay: stagger(15), ease: spring({ bounce: 0.3, duration: 450 }) }`, with `stagger` from `#nanime/utils`.
7. Write the handlers against the array: `splice` a new item in at index 2, `shift` to remove the first, `shuffle` from `#nanime/utils` to reorder, and `sort` by kind.
8. Put the handlers in a list of `{ label, run }` steps and run the next one every 2.8 seconds with `setInterval`, started in `onMounted` and cleared in `onBeforeUnmount`.
