A car that drives itself with a scrollbar. The panel below scrolls sideways,
and the car travels the road as you go, turning to follow every bend. Scroll
back and it reverses along the same line.

## What's happening

Nothing here plays on a clock. The scroll position *is* the playhead, so the
car only moves while you do, and it stops the moment you stop. The view looks
straight down at the road, so the car's heading follows the tangent of the
curve rather than tipping over slopes, and a line is drawn behind it marking
the road it has covered.

The scroll being read is the panel's own, not the page's. You can scroll the
page freely without the car moving at all.

## How it's built

`useAnimeScroll` creates a scroll observer and returns a proxy over it.
Passing that proxy as an animation's `autoplay` links the two: instead of
advancing on its own, the animation is scrubbed by the observer's progress.
`sync: true` makes that scrub exact, so the animation's position is the scroll
position with no easing or lag between them.

Reading a sideways scroll takes two parameters. `container` points the observer
at the scrolling element instead of the document, and `axis: 'x'` tells it to
measure horizontal movement. The range then comes from `target`, `enter` and
`leave`: the target is the wide scene inside the panel, `enter: 'left left'`
starts when its left edge meets the panel's left edge, and
`leave: 'right right'` ends when its right edge meets the panel's right edge.
That covers one full sweep of the scrollbar.

`createMotionPath(pathElement)` from
[`#nanime/proxies/svg`](/misc/proxies) supplies the movement. It returns
`translateX`, `translateY` and `rotate` as function values sampled along the
path, so spreading it into the parameters gives both position and orientation
from one source. Seen from above, that `rotate` is the car's heading, the
tangent angle wherever the car currently sits.

The car is authored centred on the origin, because a motion path displaces an
element rather than placing its box. Whatever sits at the origin lands on the
line. `transform-box: fill-box` with `transform-origin: 50% 50%` turns it about
its own middle, which is how a car pivots when you are looking down at it.

The road is drawn from one `d` string four times: a thick soft edge, the surface
itself, a dashed centre line, and the trail. The trail copy is wrapped by
`createDrawable`, which exposes a `draw` property, and animating it from
`'0 0'` to `'0 1'` reveals it end to end. Because the view is overhead, every
copy shares the same coordinates, with no vertical offset to reconcile.

The car and the trail each get their own observer, because an observer links to
exactly one animation. Two observers over the same container and thresholds stay
locked together, since both read the same scroll position. Each is owned by its
`useAnimeScroll` call and reverted when the component's scope is disposed, so
nothing keeps listening after the page moves on.

## Building it yourself

1. Put a wide element inside a panel with `overflow-x: auto`, so the panel scrolls sideways while the page does not.
2. Draw the road as a `<path>` with gentle `C`/`S` curves. A straight line hides the rotation entirely, so the winding is the point.
3. Author the car viewed from above, centred on the origin, with `transform-box: fill-box` and `transform-origin: 50% 50%`. Keep it in its own component so the scene markup stays readable.
4. Create an observer with `useAnimeScroll(() => ({ container: panelRef.value, target: sceneRef.value, axis: 'x', enter: 'left left', leave: 'right right', sync: true }))`. Pass the parameters as a getter, not a plain object, so the observer is rebuilt once the template refs resolve. Read `.value` off each ref yourself. Only the parameters object is unwrapped, and AnimeJS resolves no framework refs when it parses targets, so passing the ref itself leaves both `container` and `target` falling back to `document.body` with no error.
5. Animate the car with `{ ...createMotionPath(pathElement), ease: 'linear', autoplay: scrollObserver }`. Keep the ease linear, or the scrub will not track the scrollbar one to one.
6. Give the trail its own observer with the same parameters, since an observer holds one animation and a second link replaces the first. `createDrawable` returns an array, so take `[0]` before passing it to `useAnimate`. Reveal it with `draw: ['0 0', '0 1']`.
7. Read progress from the observer's `onUpdate` callback if you want a readout. Comparing it against the previous value tells you which way the scrollbar is moving, ignoring differences under about `0.0005` so jitter at rest does not flip the reading.
